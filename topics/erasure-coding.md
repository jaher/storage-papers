# Erasure Coding, Durability & Repair

Erasure coding and durability: Reed-Solomon, RAID, regenerating codes, LRC, and field studies of disk and data corruption.

| Paper | Importance | arXiv | Authors | Published | Added | Summary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Polynomial Codes over Certain Finite Fields | ★★★★★ | https://doi.org/10.1137/0108018 | Reed et al. | 1960-06-01 | 2026-11-06 | Introduced Reed–Solomon codes, the MDS codes underlying nearly all storage erasure coding since. |
| A Case for Redundant Arrays of Inexpensive Disks (RAID) | ★★★★★ | https://doi.org/10.1145/50202.50214 | Patterson et al. | 1988-06-01 | 2026-11-06 | Proposed RAID levels 1–5, founding the disk-array redundancy framework all later durability work builds on. |
| Disk Failures in the Real World: What Does an MTTF of 1,000,000 Hours Mean to You? | ★★★★★ | http://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~bianca/papers/fast07.pdf | Schroeder et al. | 2007-02-01 | 2026-11-06 | Field study of ~100,000 disks showing real annual failure rates of 2–4% (up to 13%), far above datasheet MTTF, with correlated, non-exponential failures. |
| Failure Trends in a Large Disk Drive Population | ★★★★★ | https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast-07/failure-trends-large-disk-drive-population | Pinheiro et al. | 2007-02-01 | 2026-11-06 | Google's study of 100,000+ drives showing SMART errors strongly predict failure while temperature and utilization barely correlate. |
| Network Coding for Distributed Storage Systems | ★★★★★ | https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2010.2054295 | Dimakis et al. | 2010-09-01 | 2026-11-06 | Founded regenerating codes, deriving the optimal storage-vs-repair-bandwidth tradeoff via network-coding cut-set bounds. |
| Erasure Coding in Windows Azure Storage | ★★★★★ | https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc12/technical-sessions/presentation/huang | Huang et al. | 2012-06-01 | 2026-11-06 | Introduced Local Reconstruction Codes (LRC), deployed in Azure, cutting repair reads while keeping overhead near 1.29×; USENIX ATC '12 Best Paper. |
| XORing Elephants: Novel Erasure Codes for Big Data | ★★★★★ | https://doi.org/10.14778/2535573.2488339 | Sathiamoorthy et al. | 2013-03-01 | 2026-11-06 | Introduced locally repairable codes with optimal locality–distance tradeoff and built HDFS-Xorbas, halving repair I/O and network traffic at Facebook. |
| A Hitchhiker's Guide to Fast and Efficient Data Reconstruction in Erasure-Coded Data Centers | ★★★★★ | https://doi.org/10.1145/2619239.2626306 | Rashmi et al. | 2014-08-17 | 2026-11-06 | Piggybacking design layered on Reed–Solomon codes that cuts reconstruction network/disk traffic 25–45% with no extra storage, evaluated on Facebook's production warehouse cluster. |
| Erasure Coding vs. Replication: A Quantitative Comparison | ★★★★☆ | http://oceanstore.cs.berkeley.edu/publications/papers/pdf/erasure_iptps.pdf | Weatherspoon et al. | 2002-03-01 | 2026-11-06 | First quantitative proof that erasure-coded systems achieve orders-of-magnitude higher MTTF than replication at equal storage and bandwidth. |
| Efficient Replica Maintenance for Distributed Storage Systems | ★★★★☆ | https://www.usenix.org/event/nsdi06/tech/full_papers/chun/chun_html/ | Chun et al. | 2006-05-01 | 2026-11-06 | Carbonite: showed durability must outpace permanent failures (not transient churn) and that reintegrating returned replicas is key to cheap repair. |
| Pyramid Codes: Flexible Schemes to Trade Space for Access Efficiency in Reliable Data Storage Systems | ★★★★☆ | https://www.mhchen.com/papers/pyramid.ToS.13.pdf | Huang et al. | 2007-07-01 | 2026-11-06 | Introduced pyramid codes trading a little extra storage for much faster degraded reads, the direct precursor of locally repairable codes. |
| An Analysis of Data Corruption in the Storage Stack | ★★★★☆ | https://static.usenix.org/events/fast08/tech/full_papers/bairavasundaram/bairavasundaram_html/index.html | Bairavasundaram et al. | 2008-02-01 | 2026-11-06 | First large-scale study of silent data corruption: 400,000+ checksum mismatches across 1.53M drives in 41 months, motivating end-to-end checksums. |
| End-to-End Data Integrity for File Systems: A ZFS Case Study | ★★★★☆ | https://web-wp.archive.org/web/20250916111541/https://www.usenix.org/legacy/events/fast10/tech/full_papers/zhang.pdf | Zhang et al. | 2010-02-01 | 2026-11-06 | Fault-injection study showing ZFS's end-to-end checksums survive disk corruption well but fail against memory corruption. |
| Optimal Exact-Regenerating Codes for Distributed Storage at the MSR and MBR Points via a Product-Matrix Construction | ★★★★☆ | [1005.4178](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.4178) | Rashmi et al. | 2011-08-01 | 2026-11-06 | First explicit exact-repair constructions achieving both the MSR and MBR points of the Dimakis tradeoff for all parameters. |
| Rethinking Erasure Codes for Cloud File Systems: Minimizing I/O for Recovery and Degraded Reads | ★★★★☆ | https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast12/rethinking-erasure-codes-cloud-file-systems-minimizing-io-recovery-and-degraded | Khan et al. | 2012-02-01 | 2026-11-06 | Optimal recovery-schedule algorithm minimizing symbols read for any XOR-based code, plus rotated Reed–Solomon codes for cheaper degraded reads. |
| Having Your Cake and Eating It Too: Jointly Optimal Erasure Codes for I/O, Storage, and Network-Bandwidth | ★★★★☆ | https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/fast15/fast15-paper-rashmi.pdf | Rashmi et al. | 2015-02-01 | 2026-11-06 | Product-matrix MSR codes redesigned to be simultaneously optimal in storage, repair bandwidth, and disk I/O (5× fewer reconstruction I/Os); FAST '15 Best Paper. |
| Clay Codes: Moulding MDS Codes to Yield an MSR Code | ★★★★☆ | https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast18/presentation/vajha | Vajha et al. | 2018-02-01 | 2026-11-06 | First practical MSR code implementation (in Ceph) via coupled-layer construction, cutting repair traffic ~2.9× vs. Reed–Solomon at 1.25× overhead. |
| STAR: An Efficient Coding Scheme for Correcting Triple Storage Node Failures | ★★★☆☆ | https://lihaoxu.eng.wayne.edu/NISL/Papers/Journals/star-tc.pdf | Huang et al. | 2008-07-01 | 2026-11-06 | MDS array code correcting three simultaneous node failures with much lower decoding complexity than comparable codes (extension of EVENODD). |
| Simple Regenerating Codes: Network Coding for Cloud Storage | ★★★☆☆ | [1109.0264](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.0264) | Papailiopoulos et al. | 2011-09-01 | 2026-11-06 | First high-rate regenerating-code family with simple XOR-based exact repair and minimal disk I/O during rebuilds. |


Deliberate omissions for a later expansion: Bairavasundaram et al.'s latent-sector-errors SIGMETRICS '07 paper, and Rashmi et al.'s earlier explicit exact-regenerating construction ([0906.4913](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.4913), Oct 2009, verified).
